Why the Abstract Orb Wins Technically
By using a simple div or SVG, you can let Framer Motion handle all the complex physics and transitions without loading a single image file.

Here is how you can easily map the orb's animations to the patient's interaction states using basic Framer Motion properties:

Idle / Waiting State:

Visual: A soft, circular gradient (e.g., healthcare blue to teal).

Animation: A very slow, continuous loop altering the scale from 1 to 1.05, making it look like it is gently breathing.

Listening State (Mic Active):

Visual: The orb expands slightly and gains a glowing border.

Animation: Tie the boxShadow or scale to the user's actual voice input volume (using the Web Audio API), so the orb pulses in real-time as they speak. If that's too complex, a fast pinging animation scale: [1, 1.2, 1] works perfectly.

Thinking / Processing State:

Visual: The orb morphs its shape.

Animation: Animate the borderRadius. Changing it continuously from 50% to fluctuating values (like 40% 60% 70% 30%) creates an organic, morphing "blob" effect that looks highly advanced but is just a few lines of CSS/Framer Motion.

Success / Understood State:

Visual: A quick flash of a warmer color (like green or soft yellow) or a swift upward bounce.

Animation: A quick translateY bounce.
